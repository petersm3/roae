## 1. T4's statement cost is 8.077 bits, not 8.06 (§4.1 table; §4.3 and §5 bars)

The §4.1 table gives L(T4) = 2.58 + 2.32 + 1.58 + 1.58 = **8.06**, a sum of rounded menu entries. The quantity the
section defines is the log of the menu product: log₂(6 · 5 · 3 · 3) = log₂ 270 = **8.0768** bits. With the 2.00-bit
selection charge, T4's bar is therefore **10.077** bits, not the **10.06** printed in §4.3 and in the §5 table.

- **As registered:** L(T4) = 8.06, bar 10.06.
- **Corrected:** L(T4) = log₂ 270 = 8.077, bar 10.077 (10.08 to two decimals).
- **Effect:** none recorded. A T4 result with bits-explained in (10.06, 10.077] — at N_eval = 10⁶, a hit count
  from 926 to 936, since 10⁶ · 2^−10.06 = 936.8 and 10⁶ · 2^−10.077 = 925.9 — would pass under the printed bar and
  fail under the corrected one. No T4 verdict exists, and the
  evaluator (`roae.py --prereg-h1h3`) implements the registration as frozen; a future run must report T4 against
  both bars. The other three rows are unaffected beyond rounding: T1 and T2 are log₂(6 · 4 · 2 · 4) = log₂ 192 = 7.585
  (printed 7.58), and T3 is log₂ 576 = 9.170 (printed 9.17).

## 2. The §3.5 cross-check gate compares two different populations

§3.5 gates the evaluation stream on the F4′ `dist_autocorr` row 10 of `reports/evidence/f4p_tier1.out`
(mass(A ≤ 648) = 4.789%). That row is accumulated by `solve.c`'s `score_f4p`, which is called only for leaves that
pass the C3 test (`compute_comp_dist_x64(seq) <= kw_comp_dist_x64`): it is a statistic of the C1–C5 population
with C3 applied. The registration's §1 population, sampled by `_gs_one_sample`, is C1∩C2∩C4∩C5, which omits C3.

- **As registered:** the gate compares "the same population" with two independent estimators.
- **Corrected:** the two populations differ by C3, so a gate FAIL would be expected from the population difference
  alone and would not by itself show a sampler defect; a PASS would show the statistic is insensitive to C3 at
  that tolerance, not that the populations are equal.

## 3. "Any pass/fail is resolvable" (§6.5) holds for the point estimate only

§6.5 says the 19.9-bit resolution at N_eval = 10⁶ makes any pass/fail resolvable. That is true of the point
estimate −log₂ f_eval, which is the registered decision rule (§4.3). Near a bar, the Wilson interval of f_eval can
straddle the bar, so the verdict is resolvable as a point estimate while the interval is not decisive.

- **Corrected reading:** "resolvable as a point estimate; near a bar the interval can straddle it, and the
  registered rule is the point estimate".
