## What was Terence McKenna looking for?

[McKenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_McKenna) believed the [King Wen sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Wen_sequence) encoded a mathematical structure that described the ebb and flow of "novelty" — essentially, how much change or newness occurs over time. His theory ([Timewave Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_McKenna#Novelty_theory_and_Timewave_Zero)) proposed that:

1. The first-order difference wave of the King Wen sequence wasn't arbitrary — it was a deliberately constructed signal.
2. That signal, when fractally expanded (folded onto itself at multiple scales and combined), produced a wave that mapped onto the rise and fall of novelty throughout human history.
3. The wave would reach a point of infinite novelty (zero point) at a specific date, which he ultimately pegged to [December 21, 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_phenomenon).

## What he was specifically looking for

- **Why no 5-line transitions?** This was his entry point. He saw the absence of 5 as evidence of intentional design, not coincidence. This program's Monte Carlo analysis (`--stats`) confirms it's unusual (~1 in 550) but not impossible.
- **The shape of the wave itself** — he treated the difference values (the wave this program computes with `--wave`) as raw data for his fractal transformation. The program shows the wave but does not implement his fractal expansion step (the Timewave Zero algorithm is outside the scope of this project).
- **Hidden periodicity** — he believed the wave contained self-similar structure at multiple scales. The program's `--autocorrelation` and `--fft` analyses address this directly: no significant periodicity is detected, though with only N=63 data points the statistical power to detect weak periodicity is limited.
- **Deliberate design** — he was convinced the sequence was engineered, not accumulated randomly over time. The program's `--constraints` analysis supports the structural half of that claim: zero random permutations satisfy King Wen's combined properties (95% upper bound: less than 1 in 3,333), so the structure is not chance. Whether it was "engineered" in McKenna's sense — a deliberate act of mathematical design rather than, say, accumulated refinement of a practice — is a separate question the measurement cannot decide.

## Where ROAE supports McKenna

The sequence is genuinely unusual, and its structure is far too specific to be chance: the combined probability of satisfying both the perfect pair structure and the no-5-line property by chance is effectively zero in testing (zero of 10,000 random permutations; 95% upper bound from that sample: less than 1 in 3,333 — and the pair structure alone has analytic probability ≈10⁻⁴⁴). McKenna took that kind of structure as proof of intentional design. ROAE's measurements confirm the structure itself — and stop there: whether it arose from explicit intent or from generations of accumulated refinement is outside what computation can decide (see [SOLVE_SUMMARY.md](SOLVE_SUMMARY.md) §"What we can and cannot say").

### McKenna's 25/75 odd-even split is provably a theorem

[McKenna and McKenna](CITATIONS.md#mckenna-mckenna1975) (1975, *The Invisible Landscape*, Chapter 9 "Order in the I Ching and Order in the World") observed that the King Wen sequence has "a perfect ratio of three to one; three even integers to each odd integer," with "fourteen threes and two ones constitute sixteen instances of an odd integer occurring out of a possible sixty-four." This is the **circular reading** (64 transitions, including the wrap-around s₆₃ → s₀ which has Hamming distance 3 in KW), and 16/64 = 25.00% exact. ROAE's 2026-05-19 analysis confirms this is **provably true** — and stronger than McKenna may have realized: the ratio is **forced** by C4 + C5 plus the XOR parity identity (`popcount(a ⊕ b) ≡ popcount(a) + popcount(b) mod 2`), not an artifact of design choice. See SPECIFICATION.md Theorem (Wrap-around parity is odd).

McKenna's observation was empirical; ROAE supplies the proof and confirms the result at full canonical scale: **100.000000% of all 10,525,271,997 records in the d3 560T canonical (`9a968fa2…`, current deepest) have odd wrap-around** — exactly as the theorem requires, and necessarily so, since `solve --verify` enforces C4 + C5 on every record and the parity follows from them. Which odd value the wrap-around takes (d ∈ {1, 3, 5} — parity forces odd; note C2 constrains the 63 *linear* transitions, not the wrap: C1–C5-valid orderings with a 5-line wrap exist, SAT-decided 2026-07-03, see [CIRCULAR_KING_WEN.md](CIRCULAR_KING_WEN.md)) is budget-dependent: at the 560T canonical `solve --verify-wrap-parity` measures **91.83% d=3 / 8.17% d=1 / zero d=5** (9,665,706,017 vs 859,565,980 records), with the d=1 minority growing with node budget. The *parity* itself is invariant.

### McKenna's three rules (Chapter 9) cross-referenced against ROAE's C1-C7

McKenna formalizes the King Wen sequence under three rules (his framing — he presents them as the rules its makers followed):

| McKenna's Rule | ROAE equivalent | Status |
|---|---|---|
| (1) "Absolutely exclude transition situations with a value of five." | C2 | Already in spec |
| (2) "Absolutely exclude transition situations with a value of one, except in cases where this would interfere with rule (1)." | NOT in spec — empirical observation only | **Declined for promotion**; see below |
| (3) "A three to one ratio of even to odd transitions was maintained." | Theorem (this section) | Now a theorem |

### McKenna's Rule 2 — declined for promotion to formal C-rule

McKenna observed that the King Wen sequence contains **exactly two** transitions of Hamming distance 1, and that these two transitions occur at specific positions where orienting them differently (orient-flipping the surrounding pair) would create transitions of distance 5 — violating Rule 1. ROAE's verification: the two value-1 transitions are at hex 52→53 (hamming(36, 52) = 1) and hex 60→61 (hamming(19, 51) = 1), matching McKenna's reported "pairs 53-54 and 61-62."

The weak form ("exactly 2 transitions of value 1") is already implicit in our C5 multiset `{1:2, ...}`. The strong form ("those 2 transitions occur at C2-forced positions, not arbitrary ones") would be a positional constraint not in our C1-C7 spec.

**K-pilot result (2026-05-19, `solve --verify-rule2` over the v2 11.2T canonical, 796,357,285 records):** 83.77% of C1-C5 records violate the strong form. Only 40.4% of value-1 transitions are at C2-forced positions; 59.6% are "wasteful" by McKenna's framing. King Wen is in the 16.23% minority that obeys Rule 2 strictly.

**At the d3 560T canonical (2026-06-15, `solve --verify-rule2` over `9a968fa2…`, 10,525,271,997 records):** 80.03% violate / **19.97% obey** the strong form; 44.59% of value-1 transitions at C2-forced positions (55.41% "wasteful"), with ~2.000 value-1 transitions per record. The strictly-obeying fraction is **larger at the deeper enumeration** (16.23% → 19.97%) and the C2-forced share rises (40.4% → 44.59%) — King Wen stays in the obeying minority at both scales. (Caveat: the 11.2T point is the v2 lineage and the 560T point is the v1 lineage `9a968fa2`, so this is a scale-and-lineage comparison, not a single-lineage trend; a same-lineage 11.2T/100T/560T sweep would make it a clean trend.)

**Decision: NOT promoted to formal C-rule.** The data confirms KW is in a specific minority, but the rule itself is reverse-engineered from KW's specific value-1 placements. Promoting it would add to the C3/C6/C7 family of constraints derived from the answer (already flagged in [CRITIQUE.md](CRITIQUE.md) as a methodological concern: "the 5 rules were extracted from KW and then verified against KW"). A peer-review-defensible spec keeps reverse-engineered constraints to a minimum; we already have three (C3, C6, C7) and adding a fourth would worsen the methodological critique without first-principles justification. The framing "minimize X except where it forces Y" is a stylistic preference about which orderings are "more elegant", not a hard combinatorial constraint, and we have located no independent corroboration for McKenna's claim that this was intentional design (checked: Cook 2006, which does not discuss it). The K-pilot data is preserved as an empirical observation about King Wen; `solve --verify-rule2` is retained as a diagnostic subcommand for future analysis. Detailed assessment in `roae-private/MCKENNA_SPEC_AUDIT_AND_KPILOTS_2026_05_19.md` *(private staging repo — not publicly accessible; the decision and its full rationale are stated above, so nothing in this document rests on the private file)*.

### McKenna's "9th six" — exactly one between-pair value-6

McKenna observed that King Wen has **exactly one** between-pair transition of value 6 (at the 38↔39 boundary). ROAE's `solve --verify-9th-six` over the d3 560T canonical (`9a968fa2…`, 10,525,271,997 records, 2026-06-15) finds this is **universal in the canonical solution set, not special to KW**: **100.0000% of records have exactly one between-pair value-6** (0 records with zero or ≥2). So the *count* is forced by the C1–C5 structure; only the *position* varies — the single value-6 is distributed across the 31 between-pair boundaries, peaking at boundary 20 (26.9%) and boundary 19 (21.5%), with boundary 4 (11.4%) and a spread over the remainder. KW's specific placement is therefore one of many; the "exactly one" property is a derived consequence of the constraints, not an independent design signature. `solve --verify-9th-six` is retained as a diagnostic.

**Now machine-proven (2026-07-11), not just measured:** `boundary_budget_general` + `ninth_six_trigram` in [`lean/TrigramTheorems.lean`](../lean/TrigramTheorems.lean) derive the full forced between-pair distance multiset {1:2, 2:8, 3:13, 4:7, 6:1} — hence exactly one value-6 boundary — from C1+C5 for **every** valid ordering, turning the 560T measurement above into a corollary. The positional distribution (which boundary carries the value-6) remains ordering-dependent and empirical. See [TRIGRAM_STRUCTURE.md](TRIGRAM_STRUCTURE.md) (McKenna & McKenna 1975 credited for the observation).

**Superseded 11.2T-era figures and a retired conjecture.** The earlier K-pilot on the v2 11.2T canonical (2026-05-19, 796,357,285 records) reported a much more concentrated distribution — 88.87% at boundaries 19/20/21, modal boundary 20 = 49.9%, and "never at boundaries 0-18" — which prompted a conjecture (recorded in [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md), 2026-05-19) that "never at 0-18" might be derivable as a theorem from C1+C2+C5. The 560T data above **refutes** that: boundary 4 (inside 0-18) carries 11.4% of records, so the concentration was an artifact of the shallower 11.2T slice, and the conjecture is **retired as false** (2026-07-11), not open. The count result ("exactly one") holds at both scales; the 560T positional figures in this section are the authoritative ones.

### McKenna's "closure" observation

McKenna also describes a graphical-symmetry property: the difference wave plot of the King Wen sequence, when rotated 180° and superimposed, achieves congruence at four adjacent points at the beginning and end of the sequence (his Figure 18B). This palindromic-like symmetry property of D(S) is partially captured by ROAE's `--palindromes` analysis. We do not currently formalize it as an independent constraint; it appears to be a derived property of C1+C2+C3+C5 rather than an independent rule.

### McKenna's Monte Carlo (1971)

In the same chapter, McKenna reports an early-1970s Monte Carlo: "More than 1.2 million hexagram sequences were randomly generated by computer (all sequences having the property possessed by the King Wen sequence that every second hexagram is either the inverse or the complement of its predecessor). Of these 1.2 million Wen-like sequences, 805 were found to have the properties of a three to one ratio of even to odd transitions, no transitions of value five, and the type of closure described previously." That's a hit rate of **0.07% (1 in 1,769)** for [3:1 ratio + no-5 + closure] among C1-satisfying sequences.

ROAE's `solve.c --null-pair-constrained` (10⁹ samples) measures a related but distinct quantity: **4.29%** of C1-satisfying sequences satisfy C2 alone (no-5). McKenna's stricter filter (adding 3:1 + closure) is consistent with our looser one. Both confirm KW's structure is highly unusual within the C1 universe.

This makes the 25/75 observation one of McKenna's most accurate quantitative claims about the King Wen sequence — empirically correct, exact in the circular reading, and mathematically forced rather than coincidental.

## Where ROAE challenges McKenna

### Claims challenged by this program

1. **The absence of 5-line transitions is evidence of deliberate design** _(our paraphrase — his Chapter 9 rule reads "Order among the thirty-two pairs was determined by a **wish to absolutely exclude** transition situations with a value of five"; he claims design intent, and his own Monte Carlo reports 805 of 1.2M Wen-like sequences qualifying, so he makes no claim of uniqueness to King Wen)_ — McKenna treated the no-5-line-transition property as a smoking gun of deep intentional design. Two separate ROAE findings challenge the "extraordinary / unique" framing:

   (a) **Rate among random orderings**: The Monte Carlo (`--stats`) shows unconstrained random orderings avoid all 5-line transitions ~1 in 550 of the time. Unusual, yes. Miraculous, no. It's roughly as likely as being dealt a specific two-pair hand in poker. The 10⁹-sample C null (`solve.c --null-random`) refines this: exactly 0.1828% of random 64-permutations satisfy C2.

   (b) **Not unique among historical orderings**: `solve.c --null-historical` tests 4 documented hexagram orderings. **Two of four satisfy C2 exactly** — King Wen and [Jing Fang](CITATIONS.md#jingfang)'s 8 Palaces arrangement (traditionally attributed to Jing Fang, 77–37 BCE), the latter as linearized in [Nielsen 2003](CITATIONS.md#nielsen2003)'s printed palace order (reading his palace table as one 64-term sequence, so inter-palace seams count as adjacencies, is an interpretive step; under other palace orders the seams can reach Hamming distance 5). The Mawangdui silk-text ordering (168 BCE) comes close but fails: it has exactly one 5-line transition, at its Kan→Zhen octet seam; Fu Xi ([Leibniz](CITATIONS.md#leibniz1703)'s natural-binary, not a traditional divinatory ordering) has two. *(Corrected 2026-07-05: earlier text claimed 3 of 4 and a "shared classical Chinese design principle" — computed on an erroneous Mawangdui array, now withdrawn; see CITATIONS.md errata.)* McKenna's framing of C2 as a KW fingerprint still overreaches (Jing Fang shares it, and within reverse/inverse pairs 5-line transitions are impossible), but the pan-tradition inference is no longer supported. See [CITATIONS.md](CITATIONS.md) for the McKenna and [Cook 2006](CITATIONS.md#cook2006) literature credits.

2. **The wave contains hidden periodic structure** _(our paraphrase)_ — McKenna believed the difference wave had self-similar, fractal-like properties at multiple scales. The `--autocorrelation` shows no significant lags outside the 95% noise band (+/-0.25 for N=63), and the `--fft` shows no frequencies above the white noise floor. However, the sequence is short enough that weak periodicity cannot be ruled out — absence of evidence is not evidence of absence at this sample size.

3. **The sequence encodes a sophisticated signal** _(our paraphrase)_ — The `--entropy` analysis places King Wen at the ≈12th percentile against the unconstrained null (6th against the pair-constrained null; neither survives Bonferroni correction — [CRITIQUE.md](CRITIQUE.md)) — mildly more structured than random, but roughly 1 in 8 random shuffles would be equally structured. The `--markov` permutation test shows King Wen's transition matrix is at the 43rd percentile — indistinguishable from random orderings. The apparent Markov patterns (e.g., "6 is always followed by 2") are based on sample sizes of 2-8 observations and are not statistically significant.

4. **The ordering was optimized for smooth transitions** _(our paraphrase)_ — Compared against unconstrained random paths, King Wen is at the 97th percentile (3.35x a Gray code). But this is misleading: compared against the correct null model (random orderings that also satisfy the pair constraint), King Wen is at the ~29th percentile — completely typical. The pair constraint itself makes paths longer; King Wen is not unusually rough given its structure.

### Claims challenged by published researchers

5. **McKenna's wave construction was arbitrary** — Mathematician [Matthew Watkins](https://empslocal.ex.ac.uk/people/staff/mrwatkin/) showed that McKenna and his collaborator Royce Kelley made several unjustified choices when transforming the first-order difference into the Timewave: how the wave was "folded" onto itself, how many iterations of expansion were applied, and which mathematical operations combined the scales. Different (equally valid) choices produce completely different timewaves, meaning the output is not uniquely determined by the sequence. ([The Watkins Objection](https://www.fourmilab.ch/rpkp/autopsy.html))

6. **Errors in the original computation** — [Peter Meyer](https://www.fractal-timewave.com/) (unreachable as of 2026-08-29 — TLS handshake fails; [archived copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20260704042803/https://www.fractal-timewave.com/), snapshot 2026-07-04, verified resolving 2026-08-29), who programmed the original Timewave Zero software ([history](http://web.archive.org/web/20260704042525/https://fractal-timewave.com/articles/hist.html) (archived 2026-07-04; the live host at `fractal-timewave.com` unreachable as of 2026-08-29, verified 2026-08-29)), and later Watkins independently confirmed that some of McKenna's hand calculations contained arithmetic errors that propagated into the published wave. When corrected, the wave shape changed.

7. **The 384-day period is assumed, not derived** — McKenna claimed the wave's base period was 384 days (6 lines x 64 hexagrams), which he connected to 13 lunar months ([derivation](http://web.archive.org/web/20260704042829/https://www.fractal-timewave.com/articles/derivation_10.htm) (archived 2026-07-04; live host unreachable as of 2026-08-29, verified 2026-08-29)). But this number is simply the count of lines in the sequence — it has no mathematical relationship to the wave's shape or properties. Any base period could be substituted.

8. **The end-date was moved** — McKenna originally calculated a different end-date, then shifted it to [December 21, 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_phenomenon) to coincide with the Maya calendar long count completion ([zero date analysis](http://web.archive.org/web/20260704043807/https://www.fractal-timewave.com/articles/zerodate_10.html) (archived 2026-07-04; live host unreachable as of 2026-08-29, verified 2026-08-29)). This was a post-hoc adjustment, not a prediction derived from the mathematics.

### Additional findings from ROAE

- **The wave's structure is local, not global** — `--windowed-entropy` shows entropy varies across the sequence, with some regions more structured than others. There is no uniform "signal" — the structure comes in patches. (Note: windowed entropy is exploratory visualization without a null model; apparent patterns may reflect random variation.)
- **Upper and lower trigrams change independently** — `--mutual-info` shows near-zero mutual information between upper and lower trigram transitions. Whatever rules govern the sequence, they don't couple the two halves of each hexagram. (Note: with all 64 hexagrams spanning every (upper, lower) trigram combination exactly once — a complete [Latin square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_square) — independence is expected by construction.)
- **Yin-yang balance is a tautology** — `--yinyang` confirms exactly 192 yang and 192 yin lines across all 64 hexagrams. This is a necessary consequence of containing all 64 possible hexagrams exactly once, not evidence of additional design. The local balance (where yang or yin clusters) is a property of the ordering.
- **~~The sequence is unique among historical orderings~~** [**SUPERSEDED 2026-04-19 by more detailed testing; Mawangdui half corrected 2026-07-05**] — Earlier ROAE output (`--sequences` in roae.py) compared King Wen against Fu Xi and Mawangdui orderings. `solve.c --null-historical` now tests **four** historical orderings (Fu Xi, King Wen, Mawangdui, Jing Fang's 8 Palaces). The finding as corrected: **2 of 4 comparison orderings (KW, Jing Fang — the latter as linearized in Nielsen's printed palace order) satisfy C2 exactly**; the authentic Mawangdui order has exactly one 5-line transition at a trigram-octet seam (the pre-2026-07-05 "3 of 4" figure used an erroneous Mawangdui array). C1 (pair structure) and C3 (complement distance ≤ 776) remain King-Wen-specific within the tested historical set: Mawangdui and Jing Fang have their own pair geometries and complement-distance profiles, but not KW's specific ones.
- **Complements are unusually close — scope stated** — `--complements` shows King Wen places complementary hexagrams closer together than random (0th percentile **against unconstrained random permutations** — the figure `--complements` itself computes). Under the exact pair-constrained (C1&C4) null the tail is 8.1% (`verify.py --check-null-g`); the separately measured 3.9th-percentile figure is at the stricter C1+C2+C4+C5 scope (solve.py differential sample; scope label corrected 2026-07-22). *(**Flagged 2026-08-01, lens sweep** — the 3.9th-percentile figure is not supported by the population it is labelled with; the suite's own ledger gives ≈12% at this scope. Do not cite it: see [SOLVE.md](SOLVE.md) §Rule 3.)* Within the fully constrained C1–C5 population the picture inverts: KW sits at the C3 **maximum** (see [SOLVE.md](SOLVE.md)'s C3-ceiling note).
- **Palindromes are unremarkable** — `--palindromes` under pair-constrained null model shows King Wen at the 49th percentile for count and 14th for longest palindrome. No evidence of palindromic structure beyond chance.
- **The canon split is not structural** — `--canons` permutation test shows the Upper/Lower Canon division does not correspond to a statistically significant boundary (~12th percentile for mean-difference gap).

## What holds up

- The pair structure is genuinely perfect and vanishingly unlikely by chance — analytic probability ≈10⁻⁴⁴ for a random 64-permutation (`--constraints`' 0/10,000 sample, 95% upper bound less than 1 in 3,333, is the far weaker empirical check of the same fact).
- The no-5 property is real but largely explained by the pair structure: `solve.c --null-pair-constrained` (10⁹ Monte Carlo samples) gives C2|C1 = **4.29%** — a 23.5× multiplier over the unconditional 0.18% rate. Within reverse/inverse pairs, 5-line transitions are mathematically impossible (distances are always even or 6), so all C2 work happens at the 31 between-pair boundaries. In addition, C2 is **not unique to King Wen** — Jing Fang 8 Palaces, as linearized in Nielsen's printed palace order, also satisfies it (the authentic Mawangdui order does not quite: one 5-line transition at its octet seam; corrected 2026-07-05).
- The complement distance is genuinely unusual **against unconstrained random permutations** (0th percentile; 8.1% under the exact pair-constrained C1&C4 null, `verify.py --check-null-g`; 3.9th percentile — sampled, and **flagged 2026-08-01**, see [SOLVE.md](SOLVE.md) §Rule 3 — at the stricter C1+C2+C4+C5 scope; and within the C1–C5 population KW sits at the C3 maximum, see [SOLVE.md](SOLVE.md)). `solve.c --null-gray-random` (10⁵ samples) additionally shows that **no random 6-bit Gray code achieves KW's C3 ≤ 776**: the minimum observed across 10⁵ Gray codes is 832, strictly greater than KW's 776. Even a structured permutation family explicitly optimized for adjacency (Hamming-1 transitions) cannot beat KW on complement placement.
- The XOR algebraic regularity (`--symmetry`) is a mathematical theorem — any reverse/inverse pairing of 6-bit values produces exactly 7 XOR products. It is not specific to King Wen.
- The constraint solver (`solve.c`) finds that 5 rules narrow 10^89 possible orderings to billions. Canonical counts:
  - **d3 560T: 10,525,271,997** orderings (sha `9a968fa2…`, 2026-06-08, **current deepest**)
  - **d3 100T: 3,432,399,297** orderings (sha `915abf30…`, 2026-04-20)
  - **d3 10T: 706,427,594** (sha `b85c8871…`, re-established 2026-05-13; earlier `f7b8c4fb…`/706,422,987 deprecated — see [CANONICAL_HASHES.md](CANONICAL_HASHES.md) §Deprecated)
  - **d2 10T: 286,357,503** (sha `a09280fb…`)
  All are partial enumerations at different partition strategies + node budgets; under true exhaustive enumeration they would converge. Only Position 1 is universally locked. The **number of boundary constraints needed to uniquely identify KW is 4 at d2/d3 10T and 5 at both d3 100T and d3 560T** — monotone non-decreasing with scale, with the identical greedy set `{1, 4, 21, 25, 27}` at both canonical scales *(corrected 2026-07-04: an earlier version said "4 again at 560T, non-monotone" — a survivor-counting error; see [BOUNDARY_MINIMUM.md](BOUNDARY_MINIMUM.md))*. The working-4-set count is scale-bounded (8 → 0 across 11.2T → 560T). Boundaries `{25, 27}` are **partition-stable across all four partitions tested to date** (in every greedy minimum at d2 10T, d3 10T, d3 100T, AND d3 560T). Other boundaries in the minimum set are partition + scale-dependent. See [BOUNDARY_MINIMUM.md](BOUNDARY_MINIMUM.md) + [SOLVE.md](SOLVE.md) §Boundary analysis for the full analysis.
- The sequence, arranged by people in antiquity (traditionally attributed to [~1000 BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Wen_of_Zhou), though modern scholarship is divided on the exact origin and dating), carries mathematical structure far beyond chance — zero of 10,000 random permutations satisfy its combined constraints (95% upper bound from that sample: less than 1 in 3,333; the pair structure alone has analytic probability ≈10⁻⁴⁴). Whether that structure reflects explicit intentional design, as McKenna believed, or accumulated refinement over generations is outside what these measurements can decide.

## What does not hold up

- The difference wave is not a "signal" in any meaningful sense — its Markov structure and path length are indistinguishable from random orderings that share the pair constraint.
- There is no detectable periodicity, though the data is too short to be conclusive.
- Several properties initially reported as findings (yin-yang balance, Markov patterns, trigram independence) are either tautological or statistically insignificant.
- Palindromes, canon split, recurrence rate, and neighborhood clustering are all within chance expectations under appropriate null models.

The King Wen sequence is genuinely remarkable as a combinatorial object — 5 constraints narrow 10^89 possibilities to at least millions, with Position 1 fully determined and Positions 3-18 highly constrained. But McKenna's specific claims about what the wave encodes find **no support in these measurements** — no periodicity or signal structure was detected, subject to the small-N caveats stated above — and the Timewave construction itself was independently criticised as under-determined and error-containing by Watkins and by Meyer (items 5–6 above), critiques we **relay with attribution rather than re-derive**.

## What ROAE cannot answer

The program cannot answer whether the wave "maps onto history" because that is an interpretive claim, not a mathematical one.
