# Trigram-level structure of the constraint system

Machine-checked theorems about the trigram layer of the C1–C5 constraint
system, formalized in [`lean/TrigramTheorems.lean`](../lean/TrigramTheorems.lean)
(Lean 4.31.0, core only, standalone file, zero `sorry`; toolchain pinned in
[`lean/lean-toolchain`](../lean/lean-toolchain)). This document is the prose
companion: what is proven, what is merely formalized, who each result belongs
to, and — most importantly — what is **not** being claimed.

Every hexagram is two stacked trigrams: the **lower trigram** (lines 1–3,
bits 0–2 in the project encoding, bit 0 = bottom line) and the **upper
trigram** (lines 4–6, bits 3–5). The theorems below read the constraint
system at that level.

## 1. Scope — read this before citing anything

The five theorem families make **different kinds** of claims. Precisely:

- **TG-2 and TG-3 are theorems about this project's constraint system, and
  nothing more.** TG-2 is a machine-checked property of every ordering
  satisfying C1+C5 (constraints extracted from the King Wen sequence — see
  [CRITIQUE.md](CRITIQUE.md) on the extraction circularity). TG-3 is a
  machine-checked property of the project's 48-element constraint-symmetry
  group ([SYMMETRY_SEARCH.md](SYMMETRY_SEARCH.md), TR-5,
  [`lean/Automorphism.lean`](../lean/Automorphism.lean)). Neither says
  anything about the I Ching beyond what those project-defined objects
  contain.
- **TG-1 and TG-4 are classical facts, formalized.** Nothing in them is
  claimed as a discovery; the contribution is the machine-checked lemma layer
  that the other families build on *(trust-base history: the 2026-07-26
  label correction flagged three TG-1 counts as `native_decide`; they were
  migrated to kernel `decide` on 2026-07-27 and are now kernel-only — see
  lean/README's trust-base note)*.
- **TG-5 statements are guards, not results** — they exist to *defuse*
  claims, not to make them.
- Two TG-2 corollaries (pangtong successor, flanking exclusion) appear to be
  new **as explicit propositions**, and that claim is hedged: the underlying
  relations may well be remarked in the classical or structuralist
  literature. Corrections are invited (see the standing invitation in
  [CITATIONS.md](CITATIONS.md)).

**Trust base (updated 2026-08-07).** Kernel-only end to end: finite facts
(over the 64 hexagrams, 64×64 value pairs, or 720 line permutations) are
proved by kernel `decide`/`decide +kernel` — with one exception,
`blockPreserving_iff_blockwise`, whose direct 720-permutation kernel
enumeration was measured at ~11.5 GB peak RSS and rejected as a hardware
bar, and which is therefore proved *structurally* (destructured
permutation, closed bit-scatter form, carry bounds and `omega`) rather
than by enumeration — and since 2026-08-07 the file
carries **zero** `native_decide` — the tactic that would additionally trust
Lean's compiler — anywhere; see the trust-base note in
[`lean/README.md`](../lean/README.md) for the migration history. (Until
2026-08-07 this paragraph disclosed a `decide`/`native_decide` mix; the
`native_decide` half is gone.) The TG-2
sequence-level theorems are ordinary structural proofs over every valid
ordering, checked by the kernel. Every finite/King Wen-instance claim was
additionally verified numerically in Python before being drafted in Lean;
that two-language check is repeatable via
`python3 solve.py --trigram-verify`. The Python battery re-computes the
finite engines and the King Wen instantiations; quantification over
*every* valid ordering is Lean's alone, and the battery says so in its own
header.

## 2. What TG-3 is *not*: the Hershock (1991) distinction

This distinction is stated before the results because it is the easiest way
to misquote this document.

[Hershock (1991)](CITATIONS.md#hershock1991) (*Journal of Chinese
Philosophy* 18:3) decomposes the 64 hexagrams into 14 "families of
derivation" — the orbits of the group generated by **complement, reversal,
and trigram swap acting on the hexagram set**. That decomposition is his
result. What reproduces publicly is the orbit count and size profile:
`python3 verify.py --check-classical-groups`, `THREE_GENERATOR_ORBITS=14`.
The cell-for-cell comparison against his printed appendix was carried out
off-repo on 2026-07-10 and is **not** reproducible from public material —
the appendix is copyrighted, so whether its membership sets can be
published here is an open question rather than a settled one. Any
statement about the 14-family decomposition, or about his circular
reordering built from complement, reversal, trigram swap and the nuclear
map, must cite him.

TG-3 concerns a **different group acting on a different object**:

| | TG-3 (this project) | Hershock 1991 |
|---|---|---|
| **Group** | G₁₂ ⊂ G₄₈, where G₄₈ is the centralizer of bit-reversal among the 720 permutations of the six **line positions** (the constraint-symmetry group of TR-5 / [SYMMETRY_SEARCH.md](SYMMETRY_SEARCH.md)) | the group generated by complement, reversal, and trigram swap, acting on the hexagram set. Reversal and trigram swap are *also* realisable as permutations of line positions and both lie in G₁₂; complement is not a line permutation at all, and that is what puts this group outside the S₆ framing |
| **Object acted on** | orderings (sequences) of the 64 hexagrams, by relabeling line positions in every hexagram simultaneously | the **set** of 64 hexagrams |
| **Result** | exactly 12 of the 48 constraint symmetries respect the trigram bipartition; structure S₃ × C₂; order 6 at record level | the 14-orbit "families of derivation" decomposition |

No sentence in TG-3 overlaps, duplicates, or extends Hershock's result, and
none should be quoted as if it did. The relation between the two groups is
not merely terminological, though. Two of Hershock's three generators —
reversal and trigram swap — *are* permutations of line positions, and both
lie in G₁₂ (reversal is ρ = [5,4,3,2,1,0], `rho_mem_G12`; trigram swap is
[3,4,5,0,1,2]). What separates the groups is the third generator:
complement is not a line permutation at all — every line permutation fixes
hexagram 0, and complement sends it to 63 — and Hershock's group acts on
the hexagram *set*, not on orderings.

## 3. The results

### TG-1 — the trigram factorization layer (classical, formalized)

Kernel-checked over all 64 hexagrams: inverting a hexagram swaps its two
trigrams and 3-bit-reverses each (`rev6_trigram_factor`); complementing acts
componentwise (`comp6_trigram_componentwise`); Hamming distance splits over
the bipartition, `ham(a,b) = d₃(upper) + d₃(lower)` (`ham_trigram_split`);
a hexagram is reversal-symmetric iff its upper trigram is the 3-bit reversal
of its lower (`symmetric_iff_trigram`; exactly 8 such, and exactly 8
anti-symmetric, disjoint); the 8 doubled-trigram ("pure") hexagrams are
`{0, 9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54, 63}`, closed under the canonical partner map,
forming the 4 pairs {Qian,Kun}, {Zhen²,Gen²}, {Kan²,Li²}, {Xun²,Dui²}
(`pure_hexagrams_explicit`, `pure_pairs_explicit`).

These are visually obvious classical facts. The ambient algebraic prior art
is [Goldenberg (1975)](CITATIONS.md#goldenberg1975) (Theorem 4 and the
(654321) line-permutation framing), with
[Schöter (1998)](CITATIONS.md#schoter1998) for the Boolean-algebra
vocabulary; the pure-pair placement observation is classical (Lai Zhide,
16th c., via [Schulz 1982](CITATIONS.md#schulz1982); [Wu Deng](CITATIONS.md#wudeng)'s (= Wu Cheng
吳澄, standard romanization; "Wu Deng" is Nielsen's — see CITATIONS.md) warp/weft
skeleton, 13th c., via [Nielsen 2003](CITATIONS.md#nielsen2003) — already
verified programmatically by `solve.py --books-verify`).

### TG-2 — the forced boundary budget (the lead theorem)

A C1-valid ordering has 63 adjacent transitions: 32 *within-pair* (inside a
canonical pair) and 31 *between-pair* ("boundaries"). Two machine-checked
theorems, each holding for **every** valid ordering — not just King Wen:

1. **`within_multiset_general`** — in every C1-valid ordering, the 32
   within-pair distances form exactly the multiset **{2:12, 4:12, 6:8}**.
   (C1 fixes the pair *set*, so this multiset cannot vary; it is the
   2×12 + 4×12 + 6×8 = 120 table already in CITATIONS.md, now a theorem.)
2. **`boundary_budget_general`** — in every C1+C5-valid ordering, the 31
   boundary distances form exactly the multiset **{1:2, 2:8, 3:13, 4:7, 6:1}**
   — C5's total transition multiset minus the C1-forced within-pair
   multiset. This is the project's fourth sequence-level theorem (after
   wrap parity, 15 alternations, and 30 switches) and the first with
   trigram content.

Corollaries, all machine-checked for every valid ordering:

- **The "9th six" is forced** (`ninth_six_trigram`): exactly one boundary
  complements both trigrams simultaneously (both trigram distances = 3,
  equivalently Hamming distance 6). The *observation* — King Wen has exactly
  one such transition — is due to
  [McKenna & McKenna (1975)](CITATIONS.md#mckenna-mckenna1975) (see
  [MCKENNA.md](MCKENNA.md) §"McKenna's 9th six" and
  [TR-1](../reports/TR1_EIGHT_CENTURIES_MEASURED.md)). It was measured
  universal across all 10,525,271,997 records of the 560T canonical; the
  theorem derives it from C1+C5, turning that measurement into a corollary.
  The *position* of the 9th six remains genuinely ordering-dependent
  (measured distribution in MCKENNA.md).
- **Single-line carry** (`single_line_carry`): exactly two boundaries change
  a single line — each necessarily carries one whole trigram over intact.
  Scope note, stated in the file and repeated here so it cannot be
  overquoted: carrying a trigram intact is *not* exclusive to distance-1
  boundaries (King Wen has 9 intact-carry boundaries, only 2 at d=1); only
  the d=1 count is forced.
- **C2's trigram reading** (`c2_trigram_reading`): the no-distance-5 rule
  says precisely that no transition may complement one trigram while
  changing exactly two lines of the other.
- **Pangtong successor** (`pangtong_successor`): at the unique d=6 boundary,
  the following pair is exactly the complement (pangtong) image of the
  preceding pair, in reversed order. In King Wen: pairs #37–38 → #39–40.
- **Flanking exclusion** (`flanking_exclusion`): neither pair flanking the
  d=6 boundary can be one of the 8 self-complementary pairs (the 4
  symmetric + 4 anti-symmetric pairs); only the 24 generic pairs can flank
  the 9th six — else a hexagram would repeat.

Attribution status of the last two: they appear to be **new as explicit
propositions**, hedged — the pangtong relation across King Wen's #37–38 →
#39–40 boundary may well be remarked in the classical or structuralist
literature (Schulz's and Cook's pair-relation tables are the places to
check before any publication claim). No first-discovery claim is made on
any component; corrections invited.

### TG-3 — the trigram-compatible symmetry subgroup (project-specific)

**Read §2 first.** Everything here concerns the order-48 constraint-symmetry
group G₄₈ of line-position permutations centralizing bit-reversal
([SYMMETRY_SEARCH.md](SYMMETRY_SEARCH.md), TR-5,
[`lean/Automorphism.lean`](../lean/Automorphism.lean)); the ambient
S₆ framing is credited to
[Goldenberg (1975)](CITATIONS.md#goldenberg1975). Machine-checked:

- **Exactly 12 of the 48** constraint symmetries respect the trigram
  bipartition {lines 1–3}, {lines 4–6} as an unordered block pair
  (`G12_length`), and G₁₂ is a genuine subgroup (closure + inverses).
- **Structure**: G₁₂ ≅ **S₃ × C₂** — every element is `mirrorDouble q` or
  `mirrorDouble q · ρ` for a unique within-block permutation q ∈ S₃, where
  `mirrorDouble` doubles q across the two blocks in the reversal-mirrored
  way and ρ (bit-reversal) is central
  (`G12_decomposition_covers`/`_nodup`, `mirrorDouble_hom`/`_inj`,
  `rho_central_G48`). At record level (orientation quotient) this collapses
  to **S₃, order 6** (`G6_length`, `G6_eq_mirrorDouble_image`).
- **Characterization** (`blockPreserving_iff_blockwise`, over all 720 line
  permutations — the one obligation in the file proved structurally rather
  than by kernel enumeration; see §1's trust base): a permutation preserves
  the bipartition iff some trigram
  of the image is a function of a single trigram of the input — the precise
  sense in which the other 36 elements of G₄₈ do not respect trigram
  structure.
- **Invariance and its failure**: the upper/lower trigram change-count
  functionals are invariant under the block-fixing section G₆ — `p ∈ G6`
  is the hypothesis both theorems actually carry (`uChange_mapP`,
  `lChange_mapP`). They are **not** invariant under all of G₁₂: the
  remaining coset exchanges the two functionals rather than fixing each,
  as the docstring on `uChange_mapP` records. ρ shows this on King Wen
  itself — by TG-1's `rev6_trigram_factor`, ρ swaps a hexagram's two
  trigrams (3-bit-reversing each), so applying ρ elementwise carries
  `uChange KW = 59` and `lChange KW = 58` (`uChange_KW`, `lChange_KW`) to
  58 and 59. Neither functional alone is therefore invariant under the
  orientation involution, and neither is invariant under the full
  24-element record-level group: an explicit witness σ (swap bit positions
  2 and 3, i.e. lines 3 and 4) carries King Wen to a valid ordering in the same orbit with a
  different upper-trigram change count (62 vs 59;
  `trigram_functional_not_orbit_invariant`). Methodological consequence:
  per-orbit counting under the DIV-24 gate does **not** extend to
  trigram-defined functionals; any future measured trigram battery must
  declare whether each functional is S₃-relabel-invariant.

TG-3's headline (12 of 48, ≅ S₃ × C₂) has no located external prior art —
unsurprisingly, since the constraint-symmetry group itself is
project-specific. That is a statement about our search of the literature,
not a novelty guarantee; corrections invited.

### TG-4 — nuclear naturality (presumably classical/implicit)

The nuclear map (lower nuclear = lines 2–4, upper nuclear = lines 3–5) and
its 64 → 16 → 4 image chain with terminal set {0, 21, 42, 63} are classical
(commentary tradition; treated in [Hershock 1991](CITATIONS.md#hershock1991)
as "linking" and in [Cook 2006](CITATIONS.md#cook2006)). Machine-checked
here: the nuclear map commutes with reversal and complement (`nuc_comm_rev`,
`nuc_comm_comp`), preserves symmetric hexagrams, and descends along the C1
pairing (`nuc_partner_descent`: the nuclear image of a canonical pair is
always a reversal pair or, for symmetric hexagrams, a complement pair);
image sizes 64 → 16 → 4 with the terminal set exactly {0, 21, 42, 63}
(the set hardcoded in `solve.c`'s VdB terminal check) and nuc-closed.

These commutation/descent facts are the kind of thing that is visually
obvious and very likely stated or assumed somewhere in the structuralist
literature; we have **not** located an explicit statement and claim no
discovery — the section exists to certify the well-definedness substrate
under the shipped nuclear batteries (`solve.py` V-8 `vdb_nuc`,
`nuclear_adj`). Corrections invited.

### TG-5 — vacuity guards (guards, not results)

Machine-checked precisely because each defuses a numerology claim — all three kernel-only:
`pure_pairslot_couple` via `pure_partner_bool` (`decide`), and `trigram_balance_invariant` /
`pure_pairslot_count` via `trigram_balance_range` / `pure_count_range` (`decide +kernel` since
2026-08-07 — kernel-evaluated, no compiler trust). *(Corrected 2026-08-01: this sentence
read "Kernel-checked" for all three, contradicting this document's own §Trust base ledger — at
that date two of the three went through `native_decide`. Updated 2026-08-07: the 2026-08-01
disclosure is now moot; the kernel migration made "kernel-checked for all three" true.)*

- **Trigram balance is ordering-invariant** (`trigram_balance_invariant`):
  in *any* permutation of the 64 hexagrams, each of the 8 trigrams appears
  exactly 8 times as upper and 8 times as lower — the 64 hexagrams span
  every (upper, lower) combination once, so "the King Wen sequence balances
  the eight trigrams" carries zero information about the ordering.
- **Pure adjacency is C1-forced** (`pure_pairslot_couple`,
  `pure_pairslot_count`): in every C1-valid ordering the 8 pure hexagrams
  sit in exactly 4 pure–pure pair slots. Their pairwise adjacency in King
  Wen (#1–2, #29–30, #51–52, #57–58) is forced by the pairing rule, not a
  design choice; only the four slot *positions* are a degree of freedom.

## 5. Verify yourself

```bash
cd lean && lean TrigramTheorems.lean   # exit 0 = all theorems check; expect 40 #print axioms lines, NOT silence
                                       # (wall time and peak RSS: the benchmark table in lean/README.md; Lean 4.31.0, core only)
python3 solve.py --trigram-verify      # independent Python re-check of every finite/KW-instance claim
```

The Lean file is standalone (definitions restated verbatim from
`KingWen.lean` / `Automorphism.lean`, per the one-file-per-theorem-suite
pattern) and includes §6 sanity instances for several — not all — of the
sequence-level theorems on the concrete King Wen list, guarding against
vacuously-true general statements. Two scope notes, so the guard is not
overread. The six §6 examples recompute King Wen facts directly rather
than applying the theorems, and the file carries no proof that King Wen is
a permutation of `List.range 64` — a hypothesis nine of those theorems
take — so none of those nine is formally instantiated at King Wen.
Theorems with no §6 instance at all include `within_double`,
`within_multiset_general`, `single_line_carry`, `c2_trigram_reading`,
`flanking_exclusion` and `trigram_balance_invariant`.
